THE METHOD / FROM QUESTION TO DELIVERY

Test the uncertainty.
Then commit to the work.

A clear process makes a recommendation easier to assess. Here is how we define a task, test the difficult part, and agree on the evidence needed to accept the result.

01DEFINE THE RESULT↓02TEST THE RISK↓03DECIDE WHAT TO BUILD↓04DELIVER & HAND OVER

FIVE WORKING RULES

A process you can follow.

01

Bound the discovery

Agree on what we need to learn—and when to decide.

WHAT YOU CAN REVIEW

A brief that records the goal, scope, constraints, and decision owner.

02

Test the difficult assumption

Try a small, focused test before a large build or purchase.

WHAT YOU CAN REVIEW

A test result, sample review, or feasibility finding with its limitations.

03

Keep an open list

Separate evidence from assumptions. Record unanswered questions and who will resolve them.

WHAT YOU CAN REVIEW

A visible list of unknowns, dependencies, and reasons to stop or change course.

04

Define acceptance

Write down what the result must do and how it will be checked.

WHAT YOU CAN REVIEW

Acceptance criteria agreed before implementation or supplier commitment.

05

Check the actual result

Inspect the delivered artifact or agreed evidence—not just a progress report.

WHAT YOU CAN REVIEW

A handover that states what passed, what remains open, and who operates it next.

THE SAME METHOD / TWO DIFFERENT JOBS

Follow a worked example.

Illustrative sequences, not completed engagements or promised outcomes.

START WITH

A repeated document task.

A team needs to find specific information across a set of documents.

  1. Agree on representative documents and questions.
  2. Test retrieval on a small, approved sample.
  3. Record missed answers and access requirements.
  4. Agree on acceptance checks with the users.
  5. Build, verify, and hand over the tool and its limits.

COMMERCIAL STAGES

Scope the next commitment.

ASSESS

Is the approach feasible?

Understand technical requirements, resources, costs, and risks. Decide whether further work is justified.

STRUCTURE

How will it be delivered?

Agree on architecture or sourcing requirements, responsibilities, fees, and acceptance criteria.

OPERATE

Who takes it forward?

Deploy or coordinate delivery, hand over the work, and define any ongoing training or support.

A USEFUL NEXT STEP

Tell us the result you need.

We can start by identifying the assumption that needs testing first.
